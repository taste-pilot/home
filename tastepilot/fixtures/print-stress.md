# Print Stress Test

An opening paragraph long enough to host an expressive drop cap and to run
across several lines so page-break behavior around the opening composition can
be observed in the composed print edition of this document.

## Dense reference section

| Term | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Folio | Page number in the margin | Often bottom center |
| Recto | Right-hand page | Odd numbered |
| Verso | Left-hand page | Even numbered |
| Widow | Lone line at page top | Avoid |
| Orphan | Lone line at page bottom | Avoid |

> A print edition is an interpretation of the publication, not a screenshot
> of the website — every decision on this page exists to prove that.

## A very long passage

Paragraph one of a deliberately long section that should flow across at least
one page boundary when composed for Letter or A4 so break rules are exercised.

Paragraph two continues the passage with more sentences of reasonable length
so the composition has genuine body text to break across pages naturally.

Paragraph three continues further. Good print composition keeps at least three
lines of a paragraph together on either side of a page break.

Paragraph four continues further still, giving the composer enough material to
demonstrate orphan and widow control across successive page boundaries.

Paragraph five concludes the passage, after which imagery and specialized
blocks take over the stress duties for the remainder of the document.
