# The Working Writer's Guide to Publishing Without a Designer

You wrote the thing. Now it has to face readers, and the difference between a
wall of text and a publication is not talent — it is a set of decisions someone
has to make. This guide walks through those decisions.

## Why presentation changes outcomes

A reader decides in seconds whether a document deserves minutes. In one survey
of newsletter operators, formatting was cited as the second most common reason
subscribers gave for abandoning long posts.

Consider the numbers:

- 38% of readers report abandoning articles they describe as "hard to look at"
- The average time-to-first-scroll on long-form pages is under four seconds
- Documents with a clear visual hierarchy are shared roughly twice as often

> Nobody ever complained that a document was too pleasant to read.

The craft that fixes this is called editorial design, and for a century it
belonged to people with light tables and pica rulers. It has rules. The rules
can be learned — or borrowed.

## The anatomy of a publication

Every publication, from a zine to a journal, answers the same questions.

### Hierarchy

What is the most important thing on this page? The reader's eye should land
there first, without instruction. Titles, decks, section openers, and body copy
each get a distinct, consistent treatment.

### Measure

Lines that run too long exhaust the eye. Book designers settled on sixty to
seventy characters per line, and the figure has survived every medium change
since hot metal.

### Rhythm

Dense passages need relief. A pull quote, a statistic given room to breathe, an
illustration in the margin — these are pacing devices, not decoration. A
publication with no quiet sections has no loud ones either.

## The process, start to finish

1. Read the whole draft once without touching anything.
2. Mark the natural sections and the one idea each section carries.
3. Choose the moments that deserve visual emphasis — at most one per section.
4. Pick a coherent style and stay inside it.
5. Compose, review on both desktop and phone, then compose the print edition.

## Common failures

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No hierarchy | Everything shouts | One scale, applied consistently |
| Endless measure | Eyes lose the line | Constrain the column |
| Ornament everywhere | Reader fatigue | Cut every element that lacks a job |
| Screenshot PDFs | Broken pages, tiny type | Compose print as its own edition |

## A word on restraint

The strongest tool in editorial design is the decision to do nothing. Empty
margin is not wasted space; it is the frame that makes the picture legible.
When in doubt, remove the newest addition and look again.

![A composing stick holding set type](assets/composing-stick.png)

The publications people keep — the ones that survive on shelves and in bookmark
bars — are almost never the loudest ones. They are the ones that made reading
feel effortless.

## Where to go from here

Study the publications you already trust. Notice their column widths, their
heading scales, where they place images and where they refuse to. The grammar
is all there, in plain sight, waiting to be borrowed.
